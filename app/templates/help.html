Murphy Technical Concepts

Key concepts from John J. Murphy's Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets used in this app.

Dow Theory states that the market moves in primary (months–years), secondary (weeks–months), and minor (days–weeks) trends.

  • Uptrend: successive higher highs and higher lows — buyers dominating
  • Downtrend: successive lower highs and lower lows — sellers dominating
  • A trend is in force until proven otherwise by a break of a significant support or resistance level

  • 50-day SMA: intermediate-term trend. Price above = bullish; below = bearish.
  • 200-day SMA: long-term trend benchmark. The most watched MA on Wall Street.
  • Golden Cross: 50-day crosses above 200-day — major bullish signal, often the start of a new bull market phase.
  • Death Cross: 50-day crosses below 200-day — major bearish signal.

RSI(14) measures momentum on a 0–100 scale. Developed by J. Welles Wilder.

  • Above 70: overbought — price stretched; watch for reversal or consolidation
  • Below 30: oversold — potential exhaustion of selling; watch for bounce
  • Bullish divergence: price makes a lower low but RSI makes a higher low — weakening downtrend
  • Bearish divergence: price makes a higher high but RSI makes a lower high — weakening uptrend

Moving Average Convergence Divergence (12, 26, 9). Combines trend-following and momentum.

  • Signal line cross: MACD crossing above signal = buy; below = sell
  • Zero line cross: MACD crossing above zero confirms uptrend; below confirms downtrend
  • Histogram: difference between MACD and signal. Expanding histogram = accelerating momentum

  • Bands = 20-day SMA ± 2 standard deviations
  • Upper band touch: overbought in mean-reversion context; can also signal trend continuation in strong trends
  • Lower band touch: oversold in mean-reversion context
  • Squeeze: narrow bands = low volatility. A squeeze often precedes a big directional move (direction determined by breakout side)
  • %B: position of price within the bands. >1 = above upper; <0 = below lower

Murphy's Rule: Volume should expand in the direction of the trend.

  • Rising price + rising volume = healthy trend (institutional buying)
  • Rising price + falling volume = distribution; rally is losing conviction
  • Falling price + rising volume = selling pressure confirmed
  • OBV (On-Balance Volume): running total of volume on up days minus down days. OBV trending up before price = accumulation (bullish leading indicator)

  • Support: price level where buying interest has historically been strong enough to stop a decline. Previous lows, round numbers, MAs.
  • Resistance: price level where selling interest has historically capped a rally. Previous highs, round numbers.
  • Role reversal: once broken, support becomes resistance and vice versa (one of the most reliable principles in technical analysis).
  • Significance increases with the number of times a level has been tested.

After a significant move, markets often retrace a predictable portion before resuming the trend.

  • 38.2% — minimum retracement in a strong trend; shallow pullback
  • 50.0% — most common retracement level (Dow Theory's "half-way" rule)
  • 61.8% — "Golden ratio" retracement; deepest normal pullback before trend resumes
  • A break below 61.8% often signals the trend has reversed, not just retracing

Reversal patterns signal that the current trend is exhausting and a new trend in the opposite direction may begin. They require a prior trend to reverse.

Head & Shoulders (Bearish)

  • Three peaks: two lower shoulders flanking a higher head
  • A neckline connects the troughs between the peaks
  • Confirmed on a close below the neckline on increased volume
  • Price target = neckline minus the height from neckline to head (measured move)

Inverse Head & Shoulders (Bullish)

  • Mirror image — three troughs where the middle (head) is lowest
  • Confirmed on a close above the neckline
  • One of Murphy's highest-confidence reversal signals

Double Top (Bearish)

  • Two peaks at roughly the same price level separated by a trough
  • The second peak failing to exceed the first shows waning buying pressure
  • Confirmed on a close below the intervening trough; target = trough minus pattern height

Double Bottom (Bullish)

  • Two troughs at roughly the same level — sellers failing to push lower
  • Confirmed on a close above the intervening peak (the "confirmation point")
  • Target = confirmation point plus pattern height

Triple Tops & Bottoms

  • Three tests of the same price level — stronger confirmation of reversal than double patterns
  • Less common but more reliable

Continuation patterns represent pauses in a trend — consolidation before the prevailing trend resumes. They are generally shorter in duration than reversal patterns.

Triangles

  • Ascending Triangle: flat resistance with rising lows — buyers accumulating; bullish bias. Breakout typically to the upside.
  • Descending Triangle: flat support with declining highs — sellers pressing; bearish bias. Breakdown typically to the downside.
  • Symmetrical Triangle: converging trendlines with no clear bias. Direction of breakout determines the signal. Volume should contract into the apex and surge on the break.
  • Target for all triangles = base of the triangle projected from the breakout point

Flags & Pennants

  • Form after a sharp, nearly vertical move (the "pole") followed by a brief, tight consolidation
  • Bull Flag: slight downward drift after a strong rally — parallel channel against the trend
  • Bear Flag: slight upward bounce after a sharp decline
  • Pennant: same setup but the consolidation forms a small symmetrical triangle instead of a channel
  • Measured move = add (or subtract) the pole length to the breakout point
  • Murphy notes flags & pennants are among the most reliable short-term continuation signals

Rectangles (Trading Ranges)

  • Price oscillates between parallel, roughly horizontal support and resistance
  • Can be either continuation or reversal — direction of breakout determines the signal
  • Volume typically dries up within the range and surges on the breakout
  • Target = height of the rectangle projected from the breakout level

The Murphy Outlook Score is a single number from −1.0 to +1.0 that summarises all active technical signals for a ticker into one directional verdict.

How it's calculated

Each signal is scored −1 (bearish), 0 (neutral), or +1 (bullish) and multiplied by its weight. The final score is the weighted average:

outlook_score = Σ(signal_score × weight) / Σ(weights)

Verdict thresholds

  • Bullish — score > +0.2: majority of weighted signals agree upside momentum is present
  • Bearish — score < −0.2: majority of weighted signals point to downside pressure
  • Neutral — score between −0.2 and +0.2: signals are mixed or conflicting; no clear edge

The score bar on the Analyze page maps this range visually — a bar to the right of centre is bullish, to the left is bearish.

Price Gaps occur when a bar's price range does not overlap with the prior bar's range, leaving a blank on the chart. Murphy classifies three meaningful gap types:

  • Breakaway Gap: occurs at the start of a new trend, breaking out of a consolidation area on high volume — most significant type
  • Runaway (Measuring) Gap: occurs mid-trend; signals acceleration. The gap often appears near the midpoint of the move, providing a price target
  • Exhaustion Gap: occurs near the end of a trend on high volume, followed by a quick reversal — bearish in uptrends, bullish in downtrends
  • Filled gap: when price returns to close the gap area. Unfilled gaps in the direction of the trend are considered strong continuation signals

Key Reversal Days

  • Bearish Key Reversal: price makes a new high for the move, then reverses to close below the prior day's close — often on high volume. Signals buying exhaustion.
  • Bullish Key Reversal: price makes a new low for the move, then reverses to close above the prior day's close. Signals selling exhaustion.
  • The wider the day's range and the heavier the volume, the more significant the reversal signal
  • A single key reversal day is a warning, not a confirmed signal — look for follow-through in subsequent sessions
